Our Story

It started on a playground swing.

During the pandemic, our founder Eliana Fieldman felt profoundly alone. Not the loud kind of loneliness. The quiet kind. The kind you convince yourself isn't real.

When schools reopened, something settled. Being back helped. But Eliana noticed that for most teens, the disconnection didn't end when the restrictions did. In many ways, it deepened. And technology made it harder to recover.

Loneliness taught me that connection is not passive. It is something we practice.

She started reading about social health. What she found was clear: teens are the loneliest generation on record, and the systems around them aren't built to fix it. So she decided to build something herself.

Youth Connect CLE is the result. A nonprofit dedicated to helping Greater Cleveland teens find their people, their interests, and their place in this city. Starting with the Find Your Thing Fair this fall.

Who We Are

Built for Belonging.

Youth Connect CLE is a Cleveland-based nonprofit dedicated to strengthening teen social health. We connect young people to organizations, opportunities, and each other.

You don't need to change who you are to find your people. You just need a place to connect.

Our work is inspired by the model of Pittsburgh Social Health, which centers social connection as a vital part of well-being. We believe social health deserves the same attention as physical and mental health.

Social Health First
Connection is a protective factor. When teens feel seen and included, they thrive in every area of life.
Cleveland Proud
We work across Greater Cleveland, connecting teens across schools and neighborhoods that rarely get to meet.
Interest-Driven
Shared interests are shared ground. We use what teens already love as the on-ramp to community.
Research-Backed
Everything we do is grounded in the science of social connection and adolescent well-being.

Why It Matters

Connection is Protection.

Strong social connections reduce anxiety and depression, improve academic performance, and build resilience that lasts a lifetime. This is not a soft issue. It is a public health one.

About Our Founder

Eliana Fieldman

Eliana is a junior at Laurel School and a Capstone Scholar whose research explores connection, community, and belonging among adolescents.

Her research brief, The Practice of Belonging, was published through Laurel's Center for Research on Girls. It examines the teen loneliness crisis, the role of technology, and what actually works to address it. That research became the foundation for Youth Connect CLE.

A nationally recognized writer, Eliana contributes to The Voice, Laurel's school magazine, and serves on the Youth Advisory Board for Only7Seconds. Outside the classroom, she is a varsity swimmer, avid reader, and junk journaler.

She built this because she knows what it feels like to be disconnected. And she believes every teen in Cleveland deserves better.
